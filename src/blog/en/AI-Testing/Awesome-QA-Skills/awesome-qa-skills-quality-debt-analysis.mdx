Quality debt is a quality gap with a continuing cost or risk. Analysis should identify the item and its origin, show the observed effect, and compare repayment with containment or an explicitly accepted risk. Old work is not automatically debt, and age alone is not a priority.

## Separate debt from nearby issues

Classify each item before estimating its “interest.” A product defect, an unreliable test asset, a temporary exception, and missing evidence require different actions.

| Record type | What to capture | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active product defect | Observed behavior, affected users, source, and current controls | Track the defect itself; do not relabel it as test debt |
| Test or architecture debt | Asset, origin, recurring effort or exposure, age, and dependencies | Show the evidence that the gap keeps costing the team |
| Temporary exception | Authorized scope, owner, reason, controls, and expiry | Acceptance needs authority and a review date |
| Evidence gap | Missing or stale data needed to assess an item | Keep the conclusion unassessed rather than treating absence as proof |

A ticket, old test, or unfinished task is not enough to establish debt. The source Skill asks for the item, origin, impact, age, priority, ownership, and paydown tradeoff.

## Measure the ongoing effect

Show how the gap compounds using observed maintenance effort, exposure, or detection delay. For example, reruns and triage may consume time, but a monetary estimate needs a sourced rate. If frequency or cost data are missing, use a qualitative or conditional range and state what evidence would narrow it.

Rank by business impact, exposure, detectability, dependencies, and repayment effort or benefit. Age is one signal among these. A newer gap with serious exposure can outrank an older, low-impact item.

## Audit the evidence

Record known, missing, conflicting, stale, out_of_scope, and assumptions before assigning a priority. Check that evidence covers a comparable time period, the relevant build, and the asset being discussed.

- known: cite the issue, run, incident, effort record, or control that supports the claim.
- missing: request the smallest log, time window, impact source, or ownership detail needed.
- conflicting: preserve both accounts of cause or impact until an owner resolves them.
- stale: keep older history but do not present it as current rate or control status.
- out_of_scope: identify related product defects or release choices excluded from this pass.
- assumptions: state the validation that would keep the provisional ranking in place.

Separate facts, evidence-backed inferences, candidate recommendations, and Human decisions. Do not convert correlation between a flaky suite and escaped defects into a confirmed cause.

## Example: a flaky checkout suite

Consider an example request that describes a checkout suite delaying releases while a payment-state defect recurs. The description alone is not a source record: it does not identify the affected test, release window, users, defect ticket, root cause, age, or measured delay.

Keep at least two questions separate: Is there an active payment-state product defect, and is there a test or architecture gap that makes this behavior harder to detect? A flaky test may be related, but the cause needs evidence. Do not assume the fixture is the root cause or that quarantining a test repays the debt.

### QDA-01 candidate: recurring checkout-suite instability

| Finding field | Example record |
| --- | --- |
| Object and origin | Candidate test debt in a named checkout test or fixture; origin is **UNKNOWN** until source history or a reproducible analysis supports it |
| Applicability and source | Applies if CI records confirm repeated instability or reruns; the example request supplies no test ID, issue, time window, or run evidence |
| Observed concern and evidence state | Release delay and missed payment behavior are concerns to investigate, not confirmed cause or rate; mark **UNASSESSED** |
| Impact, priority, and owner | Gather affected release/user scope, exposure, detectability, dependency, and paydown effort. Keep priority **NOT_SCORED** until evidence and owner review; assign the checkout test owner |
| Validation and stop condition | Compare test history, failure clusters, delay records, and the product defect timeline over an agreed period. Stop the ranking if sources conflict or the active defect requires a separate incident decision. |
| Close condition | Show the changed test or architecture asset, an independent behavior check, disappearance of the debt effect, and accountable acceptance. A ticket or quarantine alone does not close it. |

This record proposes an evidence path; it does not assert that the suite caused the product defect. Treat any accepted risk as an authorized exception with an expiry or review date.

## Choose repayment, containment, or acceptance

For each item, compare the smallest repayment action with containment, monitoring, or a time-bounded acceptance. Record dependencies, owner, target date, residual risk, and the evidence that will show the effect changed.

| Option | When it fits | Record and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repay | The ongoing cost or risk justifies the repair effort | Name the asset to change, an independent behavior check, evidence that the effect changed, and the accepting owner |
| Contain | Repayment is not feasible soon, but exposure or impact can be limited | Record containment scope, compensating controls, monitoring signal, owner, and review date; define escalation if the control fails |
| Accept | An authorized owner chooses to carry the residual risk for a bounded scope | State the reason, scope, compensating controls, residual risk, approver, and expiry or review date; renewal requires a new approval |

A debt item is not repaid when a ticket closes or a document is written. The source contract requires a changed asset, an independent check of the intended behavior, evidence that the debt effect is gone, and accountable acceptance. If those conditions are not met, keep it open or record an authorized exception.

## Prepare a reviewable record

Provide the item identifier, origin evidence, impact and controls, age, comparable observation period, effort or exposure data, dependencies, and repayment options. Mark each amount and rate as observed, estimated, or unknown. Keep current defects and decisions outside the debt record unless their relationship is sourced.

## Common mistakes

1. Calling every old test a debt item.
2. Ranking by age while ignoring exposure, detectability, and repayment cost.
3. Assigning a root cause from correlation.
4. Closing the ticket without evidence that the debt effect disappeared.

## FAQ

### Can quality debt be accepted?

Yes, when an authorized owner records the impact, controls, residual risk, and an expiry or review date.

### How do I show that debt was repaid?

Use the original evidence measure, show the relevant asset changed, independently verify the intended behavior, and confirm the debt effect is gone.
