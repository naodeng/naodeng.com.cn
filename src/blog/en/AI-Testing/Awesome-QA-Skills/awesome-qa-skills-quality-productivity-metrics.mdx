Counting test cases and closed tickets can make a busy team look productive while user risk grows. Productivity measurement needs to ask what useful quality signal moved, under which conditions, and with what trade-off.

## Why this Skill is needed

The Quality Productivity Metrics Skill reviews quality and delivery measures together. It distinguishes flow evidence from individual activity, exposes attribution limits, and designs checks that make gaming harder.

## What the Skill does

The Skill asks whether a measure describes a system outcome or merely rewards visible activity.

Use this Skill when comparing QA flow after a process or tooling change, designing an engineering scorecard, or reviewing whether a productivity claim has quality support. It does not rank people or turn activity counts into a release decision.

A useful finding names the quality metric, delivery metric, denominator, attribution limit, gaming risk, evidence state, and Human decision outside the analysis.

Use it when a team wants to know whether faster execution, more automation, or shorter triage improved delivery without increasing escaped risk.

## What it does not do

The Quality Productivity Metrics Skill can organize material, evidence, and next actions for Quality signal. It does not replace:

- Confirmation of rules, scope, and risk by the accountable domain owner.
- A real environment, account, dataset, log, or run record; static analysis does not become runtime evidence by itself.
- Authorization for release, compliance, production actions, or residual-risk acceptance.
- A Human decision when supplied sources conflict.

## What it checks

Review whether quality outcomes, delivery speed, and their measurement rules can be connected; activity counts should not be converted directly into scores. Start with a matrix based on the source Skill's output contract:

| Focus | Question before analysis | Handoff output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality signal | Whether the change affected user risk or detection outcome | Evidence and source |
| Delivery signal | Whether feedback became faster under a comparable cohort | Window and denominator |
| Attribution limit | Other changes that could explain movement | Candidate confounders |
| Use boundary | How the result may be used safely | Team learning, not ranking |

If a row has only a conventional expectation and no source or validation method, keep it open instead of turning it into a pass.

## Audit inputs before you start

Before analyzing Quality signal, classify the input into six evidence states. A gap is not automatically a failure, but it must not disappear inside the conclusion.

| State | Meaning | How this Skill should handle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| known | Directly supported by the supplied material | Keep the source, version, and time with the judgment |
| missing | Needed for this pass but not supplied | Name the smallest evidence action and limit the conclusion |
| conflicting | Sources disagree | Show both sources and route the conflict to an owner |
| stale | Present but outside the relevant version or time window | Mark freshness; old evidence is not current proof |
| out_of_scope | Related but excluded from this pass | Keep the boundary explicit |
| assumptions | Temporarily adopted to continue analysis | State how and when the assumption will be checked |

Keep the input version, scope, environment, evidence locations, and accountable owner together. Without a run record, deliver analysis, design, or a validation plan—not an execution pass.

## From problem to structured Finding

Connect the source, scope, evidence state, analysis, owner, action, close condition, and validation before writing the conclusion. The case below keeps this Skill’s identifier and domain context.

### Keep the decision layers separate

For Quality signal, do not compress four different kinds of language into “recommended to pass”:

| Layer | How to write it | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fact | What the supplied material directly shows | Cite the source, version, input, or run record for the focus |
| Evidence-backed Inference | What several facts support together | Show the inference chain and retain uncertainty |
| Recommendation | The smallest next action | Name the evidence, review, execution, or regression path |
| Human Decision | What an accountable person must decide | Leave scope, risk acceptance, resources, and release meaning to the owner |

## A complete case

This case follows Input, Analysis, Finding, Decision, and Validation. When material is incomplete, keep missing, conflicting, or assumptions visible instead of turning them into a pass.

### Input

Put the material on the table before asking for a judgment. Gaps can remain, but their status must stay visible.

| Material | What to provide | What to do when it is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change evaluated | A CI migration that reduced queue time for two product squads | Do not attribute every change to it |
| Quality and delivery events | Feedback time, escaped defects, flaky reruns, deploys, and rework | Keep unavailable events unassessed |
| Denominator and cohort | Runs, releases, changed files, users, or fixed team period | Reject mismatched denominators |
| Use boundary | Team learning, planning, or compensation context | Do not use a draft metric to punish people |

Use a request like this:

~~~text
Use the quality-productivity-metrics Skill.

Task: Evaluate whether CI parallelization improved QA productivity for checkout and catalog squads without increasing escaped risk.
Inputs: [before/after windows, run events, queue times, reruns, releases, defects, changed scope]
Scope: [two squads and named pipelines]
Constraints: [missing incident linkage, different release volume, privacy limits]

Separate quality metrics, delivery metrics, denominators, attribution limits, gaming risks, and Human decisions. Do not convert activity counts into an employee score.
~~~

### Analysis

Use the input, matrix, and evidence state to form the judgment before writing the Finding; keep missing material as a gap.

### Finding

#### Focused example: Evaluate faster CI without celebrating rerun volume

Suppose the supplied before-and-after summary says that parallelization reduced median feedback time from 28 minutes to 12, doubled reruns, and provides no release linkage for escaped defects. If raw CI records support the figures, the flow change can be reported as evidenced; the quality impact remains unassessed. Investigate rerun causes and add release linkage before judging the overall value of the change. This illustrates the analysis and does not report measurements made by this site.

The result can show what supplied cohorts and events support. It cannot prove individual effort, causal impact from one change, or that shorter feedback is valuable if it increases false confidence.

#### Example finding: turn one problem into a handoff

Follow the example above. If the input contains summary numbers but no raw run records or defect-to-release linkage, record the missing evidence explicitly. “Not linked” does not by itself mean quality got worse.

| Field | Example wording |
| --- | --- |
| Source and scope | The two teams, named pipeline, and comparison windows; use the actual supplied versions and scope |
| Finding | Feedback time fell and reruns increased, but the summary cannot show whether quality changed |
| Evidence state | If CI run records and defect-to-release links are absent, report the flow summary and quality impact separately; keep quality `unassessed` |
| Impact | Faster feedback may reduce waiting, while extra reruns add triage effort; this does not support individual or team ranking |
| Owner and open decision | The pipeline owner confirms run definitions; the quality owner confirms defect attribution and intended use |
| Action and close condition | Provide comparable cohorts, rerun causes, and release linkage; trace conclusions to sources and retain any unresolved quality gap |
| Validation | Recalculate feedback time and reruns for the same service scope and comparable windows, keeping the source query and release-link check |

This record tells the next owner what evidence is missing, who will confirm it, and which check could change the current judgment.

### Decision

The accountable owner confirms the decision question and risk trade-off; the Skill does not make that choice.

### Validation

Before closing the finding, run the stated validation and retain the raw artifact. Without an execution record, the status remains unverified.

## How a Finding enters the next stage

### Handoff output

| Output field | Why it exists | Example status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality signal | Whether the change affected user risk or detection outcome | Evidence and source |
| Delivery signal | Whether feedback became faster under a comparable cohort | Window and denominator |
| Attribution limit | Other changes that could explain movement | Candidate confounders |
| Use boundary | How the result may be used safely | Team learning, not ranking |

Every conclusion should point to a source, evidence state, and next action. If evidence is missing, use pending, blocked, unassessed, or NOT_SCORED instead of filling the gap with confidence.

### Next-stage route

Route the follow-up by the gap:

| Gap | Next owner | Question to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before and after cohorts or windows are not comparable | Pipeline owner | Align scope, event definitions, and measurement windows before recalculating flow metrics |
| Quality outcomes are not linked to releases or changes | Quality or data platform owner | Add traceability or state that quality impact remains unassessed |
| Results may be used for team evaluation | Business and governance owners | Review intended use, privacy, and gaming risks; the analysis is not approval to rank |

Keep sources, evidence states, and validation actions in the handoff. The next stage must not turn correlation into causation.

## How to prepare better input

Provide comparable windows, pipeline and release IDs, event definitions, changed-scope information, and known confounders. State whether the output is for planning, team learning, or another governed decision.

A useful handoff includes input versions, scope, time window, evidence index, assumptions, Human decision boundary, and the smallest validation action.

## Common traps

1. Using test count, commit count, or ticket closure as a stand-alone productivity score.
2. Comparing teams with different risk, release volume, or denominator definitions.
3. Calling a tooling change causal while ignoring flaky reruns, staffing changes, or scope reduction.

## FAQ

### Can this Skill produce a team ranking?

It can organize comparable evidence, but ranking is a separate governance decision. Expose uncertainty and gaming risk before anyone uses it for evaluation.

### What if quality data is missing?

Keep productivity and quality signals separate, mark the missing part unassessed, and name the smallest linkage or sampling action.

The Skill is most useful when attached to one real project artifact and kept with its source evidence. Start narrow, validate the uncertain part, and expand only when evidence supports it.
