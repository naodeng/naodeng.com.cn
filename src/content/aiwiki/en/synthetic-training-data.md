# Synthetic Training Data

## Quick Take

- **In brief:** Synthetic training data is generated by a model, program, or rules.
- **Useful for:** Adding examples for rare or structured tasks.
- **Main limit:** More generated examples do not guarantee better data.

## Overview

A team can generate candidate examples that pair a coding request with a patch and test explanation, then review them before training.

## Core Definition

Synthetic training data consists of examples created by software, rules, or a model for use in model training.

## Background and Evolution

Synthetic data can expand training material when human examples are scarce or costly. Its usefulness depends on how examples are generated, filtered, and checked.

## How It Works

A generator creates samples from prompts, templates, rules, or existing data. Review and filtering remove errors, duplicates, and unsuitable content before the data enters training.

## Applications in Software Development and Testing

Generate examples for uncommon languages, error cases, or consistent code-review formats. Keep a provenance record with the generator, filters, and dataset version.

## Strengths and Limitations

Synthetic samples can be produced quickly and in consistent formats. They may repeat errors, reflect the generator's bias, or fail to represent real user tasks.

## Comparison with Similar Terms

Training data updates a model. Evaluation data measures performance. Distillation may use teacher-generated outputs, but it describes a training method rather than a dataset source alone.

## Best Practices

Review samples, check duplication and provenance, keep evaluation data separate, and test on real or independently created cases.

## Common Pitfalls

Do not let the same generated questions leak into training and evaluation. Do not use volume as a substitute for quality checks.

## FAQ

### Can synthetic training data replace human examples?

Not in every task. Compare its coverage and error rate with reviewed real examples and independent evaluations.
